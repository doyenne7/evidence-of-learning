Critical AI Evaluation
Student weighs pros and cons of AI tools for real-world decisions and articulates an evidence-based position on when human oversight is necessary.
AI4K12 BI-5 CSTA IC
LevelWhat the student can do
Beginning Either fully trusts AI ("it's always right") or fully rejects it ("AI is bad"). Cannot reason from evidence.
Developing Offers one-sided evaluation (only pros or only cons); does not use AI vocabulary or cite lessons as evidence.
Proficient Gives at least two reasons (pro and con), uses at least one AI vocabulary term correctly, and identifies one situation requiring human oversight.
Advanced Constructs a nuanced, evidence-based argument; uses multiple AI vocabulary terms; proposes specific safeguards rather than blanket rejection.

Every assessment item in the Year 3 bank was built, tested, and validated through a structured 3-stage pipeline. Items are backed by psychometric data from 847 students across 12 schools over 3 years.

🔄
3-Stage Item Development Pipeline
Stage 1 — Year 1
Draft
Expert-reviewed item drafts
  • Items drafted from Year 1 formative signal patterns
  • Expert review by 3 AI educators
  • Cognitive interviews with 12 students (grades 3–5)
  • Readability target: Flesch-Kincaid Grade 4.2
→
Stage 2 — Year 2
Pilot
Field test + item analysis
  • Field tested: 240 students, 8 schools
  • Item analysis: difficulty and discrimination
  • Items with discrimination < 0.25 revised
  • Misconception data from Year 2 dashboard informed distractors
→
Stage 3 — Year 3
Validate
Full validation + publication
  • Full validation: 847 students, 12 schools
  • Reliability analysis (Cronbach's α, test-retest)
  • Pre-post effect size computed
  • Final item bank published with psychometric data

Sample Item Bank
Multiple Choice Outcome 1 Introduced: Year 1 AI4K12 BI-3
When an AI makes a prediction, what does it use?
A Its own feelings
B Random guessing
C ✓ Patterns from training data
D Hand-coded rules for every question
Difficulty: 0.78 (Easy) Discrimination: 0.42
Multiple Choice Outcome 3 Introduced: Year 2 AI4K12 BI-5
A school's AI book recommender was only trained on books enjoyed by English-speaking students. What problem would most likely occur?
A Too many recommendations
B ✓ Poor recommendations for multilingual students
C The AI would become slower
D Only new books get recommended
Difficulty: 0.61 (Medium) Discrimination: 0.58
Scenario Outcome 2 Introduced: Year 2 AI4K12 BI-3
An AI trained on 5 photos of dogs keeps mistaking cats for dogs. If you give it 500 more dog photos, will it improve? Explain why.
Scoring Rubric (3 pts):
1 pt — Mentions that more data (quantity) was added
1 pt — Explains the mechanism of improvement (more patterns to learn from)
1 pt — Acknowledges limitations (it might still make mistakes; needs diverse data)
Difficulty: 0.64 (Medium) Discrimination: 0.61
Open Response Outcome 5 Introduced: Year 3 AI4K12 BI-5 CSTA IC
Your school wants an AI to choose Student of the Month. Do you think this is a good idea? Use at least 2 reasons and at least 1 AI vocabulary word.
Scoring Rubric (5 pts):
1 pt — Clear claim (yes or no with a position)
2 pts — At least 2 distinct reasons (1 pt each)
1 pt — Correct use of at least 1 AI vocabulary term (e.g., "bias," "training data," "prediction")
1 pt — Quality of reasoning (reasons are connected to how AI works, not just opinions)
Inter-rater reliability: 87% exact agreement across 120 hand-scored responses (Year 3 calibration session).
Difficulty: 0.52 (Hard) Discrimination: 0.68

The Year 3 framework is designed to scale from a single classroom to a full district system — with tiered adoption paths, minimal technical requirements, and a sustainability model that outlasts the grant period.

🏗️
Implementation Tiers
1
Adoption Level
Classroom
  • Single teacher, any K–5 grade
  • Minimal tech — 1 device per 2–3 students
  • Uses Year 1 signal instruments only
  • Pre/post concept checks, mood ratings
  • PDF summary report at module end
  • ~2 hrs setup; zero recurring IT work
2
Adoption Level
School-Wide
  • School-wide dashboard for instructional coach
  • Facilitator training (4 hrs, asynchronous)
  • Year 1 + Year 2 measures combined
  • Misconception detection and class alerts
  • School-level proficiency summary
  • Connects to school LMS (Google Classroom, Canvas)
3
Adoption Level
District
  • Full district reporting dashboard
  • All Year 1 + 2 + 3 instruments active
  • Outcomes aligned to district CS goals
  • School-vs-school equity view
  • Board-ready PDF export on demand
  • Annual calibration session included
💻
Technical Requirements
  • ✓ Student devices — 1:1 or shared (1 device per 2–3 students is sufficient)
  • ✓ Internet access — Standard school WiFi is sufficient
  • ✓ No additional software — Runs entirely in a web browser; no app install required
  • ✓ LMS compatible — Works with Google Classroom, Canvas, and Clever SSO
  • ✓ FERPA / COPPA compliant — No student PII leaves the district's existing systems
  • ✓ Data stored in school's LMS — District retains full data ownership and control
