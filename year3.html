🎯 Learning Outcomes

5 validated learning outcomes for the "How AI Makes Decisions" module, aligned to national standards.

1
Pattern-Based Prediction
Students can explain that AI makes predictions by identifying patterns in training data — not through randomness or magic. They can demonstrate this by correctly sorting items by features.
AI4K12 BI-3 CSTA DA
2
Training Data Influence
Students can explain how the quantity and quality of training data directly impacts the accuracy of AI predictions. They can give examples of scenarios where limited data leads to poor outcomes.
AI4K12 BI-3 AI4K12 BI-5
3
AI Bias Awareness
Students can identify how biased training data leads to unfair AI predictions. They can propose strategies to reduce bias by diversifying training data and including human oversight.
AI4K12 BI-5
4
Feature-Based Representation
Students can build a feature vector by assigning numeric values to object characteristics. They can explain why two students might get different AI recommendations even when using the same tool.
AI4K12 BI-2 AI4K12 BI-3
5
Critical AI Evaluation
Students can weigh the pros and cons of using AI tools for real-world decisions. They can articulate an evidence-based opinion about when human oversight is necessary and why AI should not operate unchecked.
AI4K12 BI-5 CSTA IC
📐 4-Level Progression Rubric

Example rubric for Outcome 3: AI Bias Awareness

Level 4
Advanced
Student identifies multiple sources of bias in training data AND proposes concrete mitigation strategies (e.g., diverse data sources, human review). Can transfer to novel scenarios not discussed in class.
Level 3
Proficient
Student correctly explains that biased training data leads to unfair AI predictions and gives at least one example from lessons (e.g., Attendance Bot, fish labeling). Understands human oversight is needed.
Level 2
Developing
Student recognizes that AI can make unfair decisions but cannot consistently explain why, or confuses bias with technical errors. Needs additional examples and reinforcement.
Level 1
Beginning
Student cannot yet explain AI bias or believes AI always makes fair decisions because it is "just a computer." Requires re-teaching with concrete, relatable scenarios.

📝 Pre/Post Assessment Design

Validated assessment items for the module. Designed for K–5 readability (Flesch-Kincaid Grade 4.2).

PRE/POST · Outcome 1 · Multiple Choice
When an AI system makes a prediction, what is it using to figure out the answer?
A) Its own feelings and ideas
B) Random guessing from a list
C) ✅ Patterns it learned from sorted data
D) Instructions typed by its programmer for every question
AI4K12 BI-3 Difficulty: Low · Discrimination index: 0.42
PRE/POST · Outcome 3 · Scenario-Based
A school uses an AI tool to recommend books to students. The AI was only trained on books enjoyed by students who speak English at home. Which problem would most likely happen?
A) The AI would recommend too many books
B) ✅ The AI would not recommend books well for students who speak other languages at home
C) The AI would become slower over time
D) The AI would only recommend books published this year
AI4K12 BI-5 Difficulty: Medium · Discrimination index: 0.58
PRE/POST · Outcome 5 · Open Response
Your school wants to use an AI tool to decide who gets Student of the Month. Do you think this is a good idea? Explain your answer using at least two reasons.
AI4K12 BI-5 CSTA IC Auto-scored rubric: claim (1pt) + evidence (2pt) + AI vocabulary (1pt)
📊 Scoring note: 4-point auto-rubric (claim present, ≥2 reasons, uses AI vocabulary, correct AI claim). 87% inter-rater agreement across 120 hand-scored test items.

🔗 The Evidence Chain: Year 1 → Year 3

Every Year 3 outcome is grounded in two years of iterative measurement.

Year 1
Concept check responses + reflection themes
Revealed that "AI bias" was consistently the hardest concept across all classes. Item bank was refined based on wrong-answer analysis.
Year 2
Teacher dashboard + misconception patterns
Systematic teacher interventions (prompted by Year 2 dashboard) improved bias concept mastery by 18 percentage points. Teacher facilitation guide was developed.
Year 3
Validated pre/post items + rubric scoring
Full assessment battery validated with 847 students. Bias outcome shows d=0.84 pre/post effect size — the strongest gain of all 5 outcomes.
