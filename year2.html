🔍 Year 1 → Year 2 Assessment Upgrades

Each of the 5 lessons has been upgraded from lightweight concept checks to richer, scenario-based assessments. Below: the Year 1 baseline, the Year 2 upgrade, and three interactive example questions.

Lesson 1 · Making Predictions Scenario-Based MC
Year 1 Was
"What does AI use to make predictions?" — Single multiple choice, one right answer, no scenario context.
Year 2 Upgrade
Scenario-based question that puts students inside a realistic situation, requiring application — not just recall.
Q1. A robot is learning to sort mail. It has been trained on 10 letters. When it sees a package, it puts it in the wrong bin. What is the most likely reason?
AThe robot is broken
BThe robot hasn't seen enough examples of packages ✓
CPackages are too heavy
DThe robot doesn't like packages
Cognitive level: Application → Transfer  |  Signal captured: Training data concept, sample size awareness
Lesson 2 · Training AI Transfer Question
Year 1 Was
"Why is training data important?" — Recognition-level MC with obvious distractor answers.
Year 2 Upgrade
Transfer question: students must diagnose a training data problem they haven't seen before, applying the concept to a new domain.
Q2. Sofia trains an AI to sort dogs vs. cats using only photos of fluffy animals. Later, the AI incorrectly labels a short-haired cat as a dog. What caused this problem?
AToo many cat photos
BThe training data wasn't diverse enough ✓
CSofia used the wrong computer
DThe AI is too old
Cognitive level: Analysis → Transfer  |  Signal captured: Training data diversity, bias root causes
Lesson 4 · AI Tools in School Open Response + Rubric
Year 1 Was
Exit ticket: "Circle one — AI is good / AI is bad / AI is both." No vocabulary expectation, no reasoning required.
Year 2 Upgrade
Rubric-scored open response requiring structured reasoning, vocabulary use, and acknowledgment of multiple perspectives.
Q3. Your school wants to use AI to decide which students get extra reading time. Write 2–3 sentences explaining one concern and one benefit.
📋 Scoring Rubric — 4 Points Total
1pt Concern is clearly stated (e.g., biased data, unfair decisions, privacy)
1pt Benefit is clearly stated (e.g., helps teachers notice patterns, saves time)
1pt Uses AI vocabulary correctly (e.g., "training data," "bias," "prediction," "feature")
1pt Logical reasoning — concern/benefit is explained with a "because" or "so that"
Total: 4 pts
Cognitive level: Evaluation → Synthesis  |  Signal captured: AI ethics reasoning, vocabulary depth, argumentation

⚠️ Top Misconceptions & Interventions

Auto-detected from wrong-answer patterns. Interventions auto-generated based on lesson content.

🔴 AI Bias — Lesson 2
Q: "What happens when training data is biased?"
39% wrong
❌ Most common: "Different opinions in training data don't affect AI's accuracy"
❌ Second: "Biased data only affects AI for one day, then it corrects itself"
💡 Suggested Intervention
Re-teach the "Attendance Bot" scenario from Lesson 2 Wrap Up. Ask: "What if only tall students were marked Present?" Connect bias to lasting, real-world consequences.
🟡 Feature Vectors — Lesson 3
Q: "How does AI use feature vectors to make decisions?"
27% wrong
❌ Most common: "AI randomly picks from available options"
💡 Suggested Intervention
Re-run the food feature vector activity as a whole-class demo. Emphasize the axes and show how proximity in the grid determines AI's choice.
🟡 Human Oversight — Lessons 2 & 3
Q: "Should AI make final decisions without humans?"
23% wrong
❌ Common: "AI always makes better decisions than humans, so it should work alone"
💡 Suggested Intervention
Use the Lesson 4 AI Activity Picker scenario — show the failure case when AI only recommends beach activities for all students regardless of location or ability.
